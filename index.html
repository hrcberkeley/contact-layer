Reading this page — who the officials are, and what the labels mean

The four marks

Agreement in timeline
An entry in the investigation’s own agreements timeline. A hatched diamond means its date is recorded as approximate.
Removal flight
A transfer of third-country nationals to that country, from the investigation’s transfers table — flight, contractor and nationalities as reported.
Disclosed meeting
An entry in the State Department’s published daily schedule. The Department listing it is the evidence.
Press-reported event
An event reported in the press but not in the Department’s own schedule. Weaker evidence: a reporter’s account, dated to the earliest article about it. Where several outlets covered the same event they are one mark, and the panel lists every report.

Labels in the instrument annex

agreement · mou · partnership
The instrument type as the embassy announcement itself classified it.
ambiguous
The source dataset’s own confidence flag. It marks an announcement where it was unclear whether a distinct instrument was really being described — a passing reference, or wording that could denote an existing agreement rather than a new one. Treat those as leads to check, not as established instruments. Unflagged entries are marked clean at source.
N sources
How many separate announcements describe this instrument. The same document is often announced many times under different names; those are merged here, and the alias line lists the other names used.
page published
When the embassy page went up — not a signing date. It runs a median of 111 days later. A signing date appears only where the announcement states one outright.

The officials

Titles for those in the State Department’s published schedule come from the schedule itself. The rest never appear in it; those roles are taken from how the coverage describes them, and where the coverage does not settle a role none is given.
